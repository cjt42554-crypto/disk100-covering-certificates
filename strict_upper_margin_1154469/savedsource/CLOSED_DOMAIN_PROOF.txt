All-domain extremum reduction and exact acceptance
==================================================
For the explicitly listed distinct centers, min_i |x-c_i|^2 is continuous
on the compact closed unit disk and attains a maximum. An interior maximum
cannot have only one nearest center (strictly convex quadratic in a neighborhood)
or exactly two (strictly convex quadratic along their bisector). Thus it is
the circumcenter of at least three distinct, noncollinear nearest centers.
The checker conservatively enumerates EVERY noncollinear triple circumcenter
inside the closed disk, without assuming a numerical Delaunay triangulation.

On the circle, partition at all pairwise bisector intersections. On every
open arc the nearest center is fixed, with squared distance
1+|c_i|^2-2c_i dot x. A maximum occurs at an arc endpoint or its antipode.
Zero-center constant arcs are handled by endpoints or the extra point (1,0).
Hence the enumerated set contains a full-domain maximum; this is a continuous
geometric proof, not a spatial sample. Every enumerated entry is independently
proved covered at U by a supplied center witness. Therefore the maximum is <=U.

Circumcenters are rational, other candidates involve one square root. All
acceptance comparisons use integers/Fraction or the exact sign of a+b*sqrt(d),
determined by signs and a^2 versus b^2*d. Equality and unit-circle boundary
points are retained. The stored PASS flag is not an input to acceptance.
This is a covering upper bound only, not an exclusion of smaller radii.
