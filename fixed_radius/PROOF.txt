Exact covering radius of one fixed rational configuration
========================================================
The configuration is pinned by SHA256
84fb2a5dd9869e641f18250b8839ceb44bfe1263fa54bf588cfdb4867fb28a36.
It consists of 100 distinct rational centers with common denominator 10^15.
All statements here concern these fixed centers.

Let f(x)=min_i |x-c_i|^2 on the whole CLOSED unit disk. This continuous
function attains its maximum. At an interior local maximum, one nearest
center is impossible by strict convexity. Two nearest centers are impossible
by strict convexity along their bisector. Thus there are at least three
nearest centers; three distinct equidistant centers cannot be collinear.
The point is therefore a noncollinear triple circumcenter. On the unit
circle, partition at every pair-bisector/circle intersection. On each open
arc a fixed center is nearest, with squared distance
1+|c|^2-2c dot x. Its maximum is at an arc endpoint or the antipode of c.
Zero-center constant arcs are covered by endpoints or the extra point (1,0).
Consequently the conservative candidates() enumeration contains every
possible full-domain maximum. The source UPPER_PROOF.txt explains this same
extremum reduction. No triangulation or spatial sample is assumed.

The checker enumerates 138437 entries: 128436 triple circumcenters within
the closed disk, 9900 pair-bisector/unit-circle intersections, 100 antipodes,
and (1,0). These are observed counts, not inputs to acceptance. For every
candidate except T(24,26,33), a supplied witness center is independently
proved to have squared distance <= S=6663999696176893/(5*10^17). The source
integer/quadratic-surd checker is byte-identical to its saved source.

For T(24,26,33), the regenerated rational circumcenter is strictly inside
the disk. The checker derives its squared distance to center24 as N/D,
using the full integers printed in polynomial.txt and exact_result.json.
At this SAME point, all 100 center-distance comparisons with N/D are exact;
none is smaller, and equality holds exactly for centers24,26,33. Thus
f(x*)=N/D. The exact strict comparison S<N/D proves every other candidate
has strictly smaller f. This establishes max f=N/D and a unique worst
candidate. Since N/D<1, a zero-center constant boundary arc cannot attain
this value; every maximum is represented in the enumeration, so the worst
point in the whole closed disk is unique as well.

The positive covering radius r=sqrt(N/D) is the positive root of
P(r)=D*r^2-N. The integers N,D are coprime, so P is primitive. The reduced
rational N/D is not a rational square, as verified by integer square roots
of N and D; P is irreducible over Q. The adjacent rational 10^-60 endpoints
lo,hi satisfy 0<lo<hi, P(lo)<0<P(hi). Because P'(r)=2D*r>0 for r>0, there
is exactly one root in that interval and it is the unique positive root.
All coefficients and both exact endpoints are explicitly stored.

The proposed top candidates were found using numerical/Decimal arithmetic,
but the acceptance proof trusts only the complete exact enumeration, exact
nearest-center comparisons, polynomial identities and rational sign tests.
The stored PASS flags and numerical rankings are never acceptance inputs.
